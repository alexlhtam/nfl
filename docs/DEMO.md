# Four-minute coaching demonstration

1. **Question — 20 seconds.** “When a receiver opens, how much of the separation change comes from the route's movement, and how much from coverage moving?” Open the Engram coaching example.
2. **Metric — 45 seconds.** Show the half-second ghost positions and the Coverage Lift card. Open the four-distance calculation. Explain that the two signed components add to the actual separation change.
3. **Engram — 45 seconds.** Scrub 1.6 to 2.1 seconds. Coverage Lift is approximately +2.64 yards, receiver movement −1.54 yards, and net separation gain +1.09 yards. Show the sustained observed window through release. This is an observation about Engram's spacing, not a claim that he was the target.
4. **Hill contrast — 45 seconds.** Switch to Hill's 1.8–2.3-second example. His +1.66-yard gain comprises approximately +1.76 yards of receiver movement and −0.10 yards of coverage movement. Two openings have different geometric sources.
5. **Coaching investigation — 45 seconds.** Select a defender, add a second receiver, inspect the shared timelines, and loop an opening. Use the association analysis to choose movements for review, without announcing causal decoy credit.
6. **Evidence and handoff — 40 seconds.** Change a threshold or lookback, explain the scope, attach a timestamped observation, and export the exact workspace plus annotated image. Show that the offline file runs without installation or an account.

The submission is a metric supported by an interactive visualization. Its originality is the precise statistic and inspectable coaching workflow; its technical evidence consists of verified source data, cross-runtime numerical tests, real-play claims, sensitivity checks, and browser tests. No coach-reviewed effectiveness or causal performance claim is made.

If a judge asks whether a high Coverage Lift means a receiver ran well, answer: “It identifies one source of changing separation. The complementary movement component, route timing, defensive context, and football interpretation remain visible.”
